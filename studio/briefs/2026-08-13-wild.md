# Sprint Brief — Verdant Wilds (WILD), Sprint 1

- **Date:** 2026-08-13
- **Author:** Priya "PJ" Joshi, Senior Producer
- **Sprint:** WILD Sprint 1 — one dev, three tickets, built in order, each merge leaves the game runnable
- **Base:** Greenfield flagship. New folder `studio/projects/wilds/`, new repo, no prior tickets. Multi-sprint project — this sprint is the foundation every later pillar stands on.

## Concept

Verdant Wilds is a monster-taming RPG with the bones of a Game-Boy-era classic — tile-grid overworld, a town and its routes, tall grass hiding wild monsters — fused with survival crafting: you chop wood, pick berries, pull fiber, fight hunger, and the snare that catches your first monster is one you crafted yourself. Everything is original — monsters, names, art — and the whole game ships as static files you double-click.

## Vertical-slice contract (six pillars, multi-sprint)

(1) tile overworld · (2) survival gathering + hunger · (3) crafting/building · (4) taming + battles · (5) loop closure + save/load · (6) readable original presentation. **This sprint ships pillars 1 and 2 whole, and starts pillar 6.** Rough arc after that: sprint 2 = crafting/building on this sprint's inventory; sprint 3 = taming, battles, loop closure. Proposals, not commitments.

## Design pillars

1. **The grid is the truth.** Movement, collision, resources — and later encounters and building — all live on the same tile grid. Any tile answers "can I walk there, can I gather it?" at a glance; nothing floats off-grid.
2. **Survival feeds taming.** Gathering isn't a mini-game bolted on — wood and fiber are the supply chain that will become snares and camps. Sprint 1 builds that economy's first turn: gather, manage hunger, eat.
3. **Original and readable.** Structure homage, zero asset theft: original monsters, names, and art only, drawn by our own code onto canvas. Readable is a shipping bar, not a vibe — it gets checked on camera by someone who didn't build it.

## Ship constraint (non-negotiable)

Plain static files in `studio/projects/wilds/` — `index.html`, `css/`, `js/` — plain script tags (no ES modules; they die on `file://`), no build step, no server code, no network, no audio. All art is code-drawn at load into offscreen canvases — no image files, no ripped assets, no external fonts. If a change breaks "double-click and play," it doesn't merge.

## Standing lessons carried from PONG Sprint 1

- **Constants block from day one (Devon):** every tunable — step time, drain rate, yields, respawn timers — in one named block. Tuning is a knob turn, not surgery.
- **Rig-honest acceptance (PJ enforces):** every "done when" passes the planning check "can our environment execute this line as written?" If a line proves unexecutable mid-sprint, we amend it on the record — nothing gets signed off "verified by construction."
- **Versioned harness (Elena):** a headless Node harness drives the real, unmodified game files from ticket 1, checked in under `wilds/tests/`, never referenced by `index.html`.
- **Human ritual:** before the sprint closes, a human double-clicks `index.html` from disk and plays the full walk–gather–eat loop with their own hands.

## This sprint ships

Three tickets, one dev, built sequentially; each ticket ends runnable. If the sprint runs short, **WILD-3 is the cut** — the game stays playable on placeholder tiles.

### WILD-1 — Walkable original overworld
Two hand-authored tile maps as data arrays — a town (working name: Mosshollow) and one route (working name: Bramble Run), each at least 20×15 tiles — connected by a walk-off-the-edge transition both ways. Grid-locked movement in the Game-Boy idiom: four directions, tap turns facing, hold steps tile-to-tile with smooth interpolation, step time a knob, no diagonals. Solid tiles (water, trees, building facades, fences) block; the camera follows the player and clamps to map bounds. Placeholder-tier original tiles are fine here as long as walkable-vs-solid reads. Tall-grass tiles may be painted on the route as terrain only — they do nothing this sprint. **Done when:** the harness proves 100k random-walk frames with zero solid overlaps and zero out-of-bounds camera frames, a scripted walk crosses town→route→town through the transition, and a live foreground session shows tile-locked stepping with the measured fps and window size recorded on the ticket.

### WILD-2 — Gathering, inventory, hunger
An interact key targets the faced tile: trees take 3 chops → 2 wood and show a depleted state; berry bushes → 2 berries; fiber plants → 1 fiber; depleted nodes keep their collision footprint and respawn after 60s (all knobs). Inventory panel on a key: cursor-driven list with counts; "use" on a berry eats it — this panel is deliberately the menu skeleton sprint 2's craft menu will reuse. Hunger on the HUD: drains full→empty in ~180s of play, a berry restores 25%, and at zero the player walks at half speed while the meter flashes — no fainting, no death; respawn-at-camp belongs to loop closure, which needs a camp, which needs crafting. Controls hint on the HUD. **Done when:** the harness proves drain rate, restore value, yields, and respawn timing to spec over 10 simulated minutes with counts never negative or NaN, and a live session gathers all three resources, rides hunger to famished, and eats back to full, captured on camera.

### WILD-3 — Original readable presentation
Replace placeholders with an original 16×16 pixel tileset drawn by code into an offscreen atlas at load — grass, tall grass, path, water, tree/stump, berry bush and fiber plant in full and depleted states, facades, fence — rendered at integer scale. Original player character with four facings and a 2-frame walk cycle that steps in time with tile movement, no sliding. HUD (hunger meter, resource counts, controls hint) in one consistent panel style. **Done when:** a full-map screenshot passes an on-the-record readability check — Marcus or Quinn, not the author, names every tile class correctly at a glance — the walk cycle shows zero slide in live capture, fps holds within the rig's measured window with the full tileset live, and the from-disk human ritual (walk town→route, gather all three, famish, eat back) is witnessed by human eyes.

## Out of scope (do not build)

- Monsters, encounters, battles, starters, snares, tall-grass logic — pillar 4; tall grass is paint this sprint
- Craft menu, campfire, cooking, fence/shelter placement — pillar 3, next sprint
- Save/load, fainting, respawn, camp — pillar 5
- Audio of any kind (studio-head directive)
- NPCs, dialogue, shops, story; building interiors — doors are solid facades
- A second route, caves, ledges, water traversal, day/night, weather
- Procedural generation — maps are hand-authored data
- Title screen, settings, key rebinding; touch, gamepad, mouse-driven play
- Frameworks, bundlers, TypeScript, image-asset pipelines — see ship constraint

If it isn't in a ticket above, it's a proposal for sprint 2, not a commitment in this one.
