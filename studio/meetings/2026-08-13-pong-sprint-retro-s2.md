Planning promoted it to a gate on the first PONG-4 commit, same-morning notes on the board. It is now two sprints, and the cheapest task this studio has — five minutes and a laptop — is the only thing standing between a finished, merged feature and done. The rig was never involved; nobody gets to blame it. I didn't do the work, so PONG-4's five points didn't bank, and that miss is mine, not Devon's — he built the whole commitment. My planning hills keep shipping verbatim and my board footprint is still zero comments. That changes before sprint 3 planning opens or the chair should say so in my review.

**PJ:** Three record-keeping failures, all mine to own. First, the gate: Decision 6(c) was written and nobody stood at it — Devon's first PONG-4 commit went in with no Marcus evidence on the board, and I, as chair, let the sequencing slide, which is how a 5-point ticket ended the sprint hostage to a missing comment. Second, Decision 4's amendment to PONG-2 — my draft, Sam's post, Quinn's wording check — was never posted; the substance survived only because it was baked into PONG-4's acceptance text, which is luck, not process. Third, I signed a ticket whose desc ("no new files beyond index/css/js") contradicts its own acceptance (a checked-in harness); Elena caught it in review and read it correctly, but ticket text should never need a reviewer's charitable reading. The satellite records drifted too: the sprint 2 standup cites PONG-3's merge hash for PONG-4's hand-off and puts the build summary under Sam's name, and Sam's per-ticket creation notes didn't happen. The board stayed true; everything orbiting it wobbled.

**Elena:** My day-zero commitment landed late and somewhere else. The harness was promised at `studio/tools/pong-harness/` before the first PONG-4 commit; it actually landed at `tests/harness.mjs` mid-review, riding the merge. The outcome exceeded the ask — versioned, load-bearing, 206 checks, re-runnable by anyone — but "late and in a different place" is exactly the pattern I flag in other people's work, and it's what created the desc-versus-acceptance contradiction PJ just owned. Also for the backlog, on the record: no key handler checks `e.repeat`, so physically holding P for ~500ms flickers pause via OS key-repeat. Pre-existing pattern, consistent house style, non-blocking — but it's a wart, and next sprint it should be a ticket, not a comment.

**Quinn:** I spent real hours this sprint proving what my evidence is *not* — that a scripted reactive bot dispatching real KeyboardEvents is not a human hand — and the acceptance line still, correctly, wants the human. Fine. But "mid-skill tester" is doing a lot of undefined work in our acceptance language: my first Ruthless run burned 61 seconds on a pure center-tracking policy that can't lose or win, and the fix was defining "mid-skill" more honestly (track plus basic aim). Write the tester policy into the ticket next time; I shouldn't be inventing the player at midnight. And the from-disk line rode the localhost-equivalence precedent on every single ticket again — Elena pre-adjudicated it each time so nothing was self-waived, but a permanent precedent is just a rule we haven't written down.

**Devon:** I built 8 points and the scoreboard credits 3, and I endorse the scoreboard — done means evidence, and the evidence isn't there. What I'd change: a dependency that can strand a ticket in qa should be visible as a blocker the day the ticket is cut, not discovered as a missing layer at close. PONG-4's acceptance named Marcus's pass from day one; the board just had no way to scream that it hadn't happened yet. Give gating work its own board presence next sprint so idle-in-qa is loud instead of quiet.
